Ordering a custom polo design for a team, club or company goes faster and lands closer to right on the first attempt when the brief is specific. None of what's needed requires any design skill — just a bit of preparation.

## 1. Your logo or existing branding

If you have a logo, send the actual source file if you have one (Ai, EPS, SVG, or at minimum a high-resolution PNG) rather than a small image pulled off a website or screenshotted from somewhere. A low-resolution logo limits what a designer can do with it cleanly.

## 2. The garment colour (or colours)

Design that's approved against white can look completely different once placed on the actual garment colour. If you already know the polo will be navy, forest green, or a specific team colour, say so upfront — it changes how the design should be built, not just how it's finally applied.

## 3. Where the design goes

Left chest only? Chest and back? A sleeve detail? Being specific about placement, even roughly, shapes how the design gets built — a design meant for a small chest placement is approached differently than one meant to be a large back-panel graphic.

## 4. The production method, if you know it

Screen print, embroidery, or sublimation, if you already know which one you're using. If you don't know yet, that's fine too — just say so, and mention it as soon as you do decide, since it can affect the design.

## 5. Any reference designs you like

Two or three polo designs — even from other brands or teams — that you like the feel of, with a note on what specifically appeals to you (the boldness, the colour palette, how minimal or busy it is). This communicates far more precisely than a written description alone.

## 6. Quantity and whether multiple colourways are needed

If the design needs to work across several garment colours (a team with home and away colours, for example), mention that upfront — it's a different scope than a single-colourway design.

## What a complete brief looks like

"We're a golf club with navy and white as our colours. Logo attached as a vector file. We want it on the left chest, small, and a larger version of a simplified motif on the back. This will be embroidered on the chest and screen printed on the back. We like the feel of [reference 1] and [reference 2] — bold but not too busy."

That's everything a designer needs to start with an accurate first concept instead of guessing.
