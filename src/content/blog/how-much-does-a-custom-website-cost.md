Ask three people what a website costs and you'll get three wildly different answers back. All of them can be technically correct too, because "a website" covers everything from a two hundred dollar DIY template to a thirty thousand dollar custom web application. The number only starts making sense once you know what's actually getting built.

## The rough price bands

A DIY builder, Wix, Squarespace, that whole category, runs roughly a hundred to five hundred dollars a year, mostly subscription fees. You're building it yourself with a drag-and-drop editor. WordPress with a freelancer typically lands between two thousand and eight thousand dollars for a professionally designed site sitting on top of WordPress, plus ongoing hosting and plugin costs that keep going after launch. A fully custom-coded site generally runs three thousand to fifteen thousand dollars or more depending on how many pages and how much complexity you need, built from scratch instead of on a pre-made platform. Custom web applications, booking systems, client portals, anything beyond a simple marketing site, often start around four thousand dollars and scale up from there based on the specific functionality involved.

## What actually moves the price within each band

The number of pages, and how much content needs writing or organizing, not just designing, matters a lot. Whether you need to edit the site yourself afterward changes things too. A site wired up with a content system you can update is more upfront work than a static one only a developer can touch. Custom functionality, a booking calendar, a client login, a payment system, costs more than a site that's purely informational and just sits there looking good. Design from scratch versus adapting an existing template takes longer, and that time shows up in the price.

## Why "custom" often costs more, and why that's sometimes worth it

A builder is cheaper because you're doing the assembly yourself inside a pre-made system, one shared across millions of other sites, which means it can't be tailored past a certain point no matter how hard you try. Custom development costs more because someone is building your specific site, with your specific structure, instead of fitting your business into a template built for everyone else too. Whether that difference is worth paying for depends on how much you genuinely need something a template can't do, and for a lot of small businesses starting out, it honestly isn't necessary yet.

## What often goes missing from the first quote you see

Ask specifically about hosting, where the site actually lives, which isn't included in a one-time build price. Ask about a domain name too, usually ten to twenty dollars a year, billed separately from everything else. Ask about ongoing maintenance, security updates, backups, and whether revisions past the first draft cost extra on top of the quoted number. A quote that only covers the build, with no mention of what happens after launch, only tells you half the real cost.

## A more useful question than "how much"

"What am I getting for this price, and what happens after launch if something needs changing?" gets you a far more useful answer than the raw number alone. Two very different sites can cost the same amount, and two nearly identical sites can cost wildly different amounts depending entirely on what's actually included in each quote.
