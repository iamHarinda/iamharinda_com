"I need a website" is the start of a conversation, not enough for anyone to quote accurately. The good news: describing what you actually need doesn't require technical language — it requires a bit of structure, and this is a template for that structure.

## 1. What the site is for

Not the pages — the purpose. Is it mainly to bring in new customers who find you searching? To give existing customers a place to book or pay? To act as a professional-looking reference point people check before deciding to work with you? This single sentence shapes almost everything else about the project.

## 2. Who's going to visit it

Local customers searching nearby? People referred by word of mouth checking you out before reaching out? Other businesses, if you're B2B? Knowing the audience shapes tone, content, and sometimes even which features matter (a booking calendar makes sense for direct consumers; a case-study-heavy site makes more sense for B2B).

## 3. What it needs to actually do

Beyond "look good" — list the actual functions: a contact form, a way to show pricing, a booking calendar, a blog you'll update, a way for people to pay online, a gallery of past work. Being specific here, even in plain language, is what turns a vague quote into an accurate one.

## 4. What you'll manage yourself vs. hand off

Will you be updating content regularly, or is this closer to "build it once and it mostly stays the same"? This single answer often determines whether you need a CMS at all.

## 5. Examples you like

Two or three websites — any industry — that you like the feel of, with a note on what specifically appeals to you about each one. This communicates far more precisely than describing a design in the abstract.

## 6. Your rough budget and timeline

Even an approximate range ("under $2,000, need it live within a month") helps a developer propose something realistic instead of guessing at what you can actually work with.

## What this looks like put together

"I run a small bakery and want a site that brings in local customers searching for custom cakes. Most visitors will be nearby people checking us out before calling. I need a way to show our cake gallery, a contact form, and pricing info — I'll want to add new photos myself every month or so. I like the feel of [site A] and [site B], especially how simple they are to navigate. Budget is flexible up to about $2,000, and I'd like it live within six weeks."

That's a complete, non-technical brief a developer can quote accurately from — no jargon required, just clarity.
