"I need a website" is the start of a conversation, not really enough for anyone to quote accurately off of. The good news is that describing what you actually need doesn't require technical language at all. It just needs a bit of structure, and here's a template for building that structure yourself.

## What the site is actually for

Not the pages, the purpose behind them. Is it mainly to bring in new customers who find you searching online? To give existing customers a place to book or pay? To act as a professional-looking reference point people check before deciding whether to work with you? This single sentence shapes almost everything else about the project that follows.

## Who's actually going to visit it

Local customers searching nearby? People referred by word of mouth checking you out before reaching out directly? Other businesses, if you're B2B? Knowing the audience shapes tone, content, and sometimes even which features matter most. A booking calendar makes sense for direct consumers. A case-study-heavy site makes more sense for B2B relationships.

## What it needs to actually do

Beyond "look good," list the actual functions. A contact form, a way to show pricing, a booking calendar, a blog you'll update, a way for people to pay online, a gallery of past work. Being specific here, even in totally plain language, is what turns a vague quote into an accurate one you can actually trust.

## What you'll manage yourself versus hand off

Will you be updating content regularly, or is this closer to "build it once and it mostly stays the same for years"? This single answer often determines whether you need a CMS at all, which changes both the cost and the build time.

## Examples you actually like

Two or three websites, any industry at all, that you like the feel of, with a note on what specifically appeals to you about each one. This communicates far more precisely than trying to describe a design in the abstract, no matter how good you are with words.

## Your rough budget and timeline

Even an approximate range, under two thousand dollars, need it live within a month, helps a developer propose something realistic instead of guessing at what you can actually work with going in.

## What this looks like all put together

"I run a small bakery and want a site that brings in local customers searching for custom cakes. Most visitors will be nearby people checking us out before calling. I need a way to show our cake gallery, a contact form, and pricing info. I'll want to add new photos myself every month or so. I like the feel of these two sites, especially how simple they are to navigate around. Budget is flexible up to about two thousand dollars, and I'd like it live within six weeks."

That's a complete, non-technical brief a developer can quote accurately from, no jargon required anywhere, just clarity about what you actually want.
