Wedding photography packages vary studio to studio, but couples comparing quotes are usually trying to answer the same underlying question: what am I actually getting for this price, and what will cost more later? Being explicit about package structure — especially around editing — is what prevents that question from turning into a mid-project dispute.

## What's typically included in a base package

- **Coverage hours** — the core variable most packages are priced around, from a few hours to full-day coverage.
- **A shooter** (and sometimes a second shooter, though this is often an add-on rather than standard).
- **Editing of the full delivered gallery** — colour correction and basic retouching on every image that makes the final cut, as distinct from the extensive retouching sometimes reserved for a smaller set of hero images.
- **Online gallery delivery** — a client-facing platform (rather than a raw folder dump) for viewing, downloading and sometimes ordering prints.
- **A defined turnaround window** — stated in the contract, not left implicit.

## What's commonly an add-on rather than standard

- **A second shooter** for wider coverage or a second angle during the ceremony.
- **An engagement session**, often bundled at a discount rather than fully separate.
- **A physical album** — design and printing are usually priced and timed separately from digital delivery.
- **Rush editing** — a faster-than-standard turnaround for an extra fee.
- **Extensive retouching beyond the standard edit** — detailed skin work, object removal, or composite images (removing a passerby, combining a group shot where someone blinked).

## Where editing decisions affect the price and the deliverable

Two studios can offer the same coverage hours and land at very different prices because of what "editing" actually means in each package. "Fully edited gallery" from one studio might mean an AI-assisted bulk colour pass on every image; from another, it might mean every image hand-corrected with a subset professionally retouched. Neither is wrong, but the difference is worth stating clearly in the package description — it's usually the actual driver of price differences that otherwise look unexplained to a couple comparing quotes.

## A clear way to describe it to clients

Rather than a vague "professional editing included," specify:

1. What every delivered photo gets (e.g. "colour correction and light retouching on every image").
2. What a smaller set gets, if applicable (e.g. "20 hero images receive detailed retouching").
3. The turnaround window for the full gallery, and separately, whether a sneak peek is included.
4. What counts as an extra (rush delivery, additional retouching requests beyond the standard).

Clients rarely object to a well-explained structure — they object to a structure that only becomes clear after they've already paid.

## Where outsourcing fits into package math

If editing is the bottleneck that limits how many weddings a studio can book, outsourcing a portion of it (the standard corrections across the full gallery, while keeping hands-on retouching for hero images in-house, or vice versa) is often what makes it possible to raise booking volume without changing what's actually promised in the package.
