A website quote often reads like a one-time cost. Pay once, own it forever, no further thought needed. In reality, a live website carries ongoing costs baked into what it actually is, and not knowing about them upfront is exactly how business owners end up surprised a year down the road when the bills start arriving.

## The costs that continue no matter what platform you pick

Domain renewal, typically ten to twenty dollars a year, due annually, though you can usually pay several years ahead if you want to avoid thinking about it yearly. Miss this and your site can go down entirely, or worse, the domain can become available for someone else to grab and hold hostage. Hosting, where the site's files actually live, billed monthly or annually depending on the provider. Ranges from a few dollars a month for a simple site to significantly more for anything with heavier traffic or custom infrastructure behind it. An SSL certificate, the security certificate that makes a site load as "https" instead of throwing a browser warning at visitors trying to reach you. Often bundled free with modern hosting, but worth confirming it's actually included and set to auto-renew on its own without manual intervention.

## Costs specific to certain platforms

WordPress brings premium plugin subscriptions, a booking plugin, a form builder, SEO tools, theme licence renewals, and periodically, developer time to sort out plugin conflicts or handle updates that go sideways unexpectedly. Website builders like Wix and Squarespace bring a monthly or annual platform subscription that covers hosting bundled in, but locks you to that platform's pricing and any price increases they decide to roll out over time without much say from you. Custom-coded sites generally carry the fewest ongoing platform fees, since there's no licensing or subscription tied to the code itself, mainly just hosting and the domain sitting on top of a clean foundation.

## The cost that's easy to underestimate, your own time or a maintenance plan

Content needs updating regularly to stay relevant. Security patches need applying, especially on WordPress where this matters more than most people realize. Something occasionally breaks and needs fixing, usually at the worst possible moment when you're busy with something else entirely. This is either your own time, if you're comfortable handling it yourself, or a maintenance arrangement with a developer, typically a flat monthly rate covering routine updates and small fixes without a separate invoice landing every time something comes up unexpectedly.

## A realistic yearly budget range

For a straightforward small business site, domain at ten to twenty dollars a year, hosting at sixty to three hundred dollars a year depending on platform and traffic levels, plus, if you want it, a maintenance plan commonly running a hundred to three hundred dollars a month depending on scope, covers most of what a well-maintained site actually costs beyond the initial build price. A larger site or one with more moving parts scales up from there naturally as complexity grows.

## Comparing three years of total cost, not just the launch price

A cheap builder subscription at twenty dollars a month adds up to over seven hundred dollars across three years, on top of whatever design work you paid for initially. A custom site with modest hosting and no ongoing subscription fees might cost more upfront but less over that same three-year window. Running this simple math before deciding helps clarify which option is actually cheaper over the timeframe you actually care about, rather than just comparing the number you see on day one.

## Why this matters when comparing quotes

Two developers quoting the same build price can leave you with very different total costs over three years, depending on what platform they build on and what ongoing fees come bundled with it by default. Ask specifically what a realistic yearly cost looks like after launch, not just the number for the initial build itself. That's the number that actually determines what a website costs you over time, not the headline price you see first in a quote.

Ongoing maintenance here is a stated flat monthly rate, no surprise invoices, listed right alongside the build pricing from the start.
