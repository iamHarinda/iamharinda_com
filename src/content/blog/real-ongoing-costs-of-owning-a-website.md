A website quote often reads like a one-time cost. Pay once, own it forever. In reality, a live website carries ongoing costs baked into what it actually is, and not knowing about them upfront is exactly how business owners end up surprised a year down the road.

## The costs that continue no matter what platform you pick

Domain renewal, typically ten to twenty dollars a year, due annually, though you can usually pay several years ahead if you want. Miss this and your site can go down entirely, or worse, the domain can become available for someone else to grab. Hosting, where the site's files actually live, billed monthly or annually. Ranges from a few dollars a month for a simple site to significantly more for anything with heavier traffic or custom infrastructure behind it. An SSL certificate, the security certificate that makes a site load as "https" instead of throwing a browser warning at visitors. Often bundled free with modern hosting, but worth confirming it's actually included and set to auto-renew on its own.

## Costs specific to certain platforms

WordPress brings premium plugin subscriptions, a booking plugin, a form builder, SEO tools, theme licence renewals, and periodically, developer time to sort out plugin conflicts or handle updates that go sideways. Website builders like Wix and Squarespace bring a monthly or annual platform subscription that covers hosting bundled in, but locks you to that platform's pricing and any price increases they decide to roll out over time. Custom-coded sites generally carry the fewest ongoing platform fees, since there's no licensing or subscription tied to the code itself, mainly just hosting and the domain sitting on top.

## The cost that's easy to underestimate, your own time or a maintenance plan

Content needs updating. Security patches need applying, especially on WordPress. Something occasionally breaks and needs fixing, usually at the worst possible moment. This is either your own time, if you're comfortable handling it yourself, or a maintenance arrangement with a developer, typically a flat monthly rate covering routine updates and small fixes without a separate invoice landing every time something comes up.

## A realistic yearly budget range

For a straightforward small business site, domain at ten to twenty dollars a year, hosting at sixty to three hundred dollars a year depending on platform and traffic, plus, if you want it, a maintenance plan commonly running a hundred to three hundred dollars a month depending on scope, covers most of what a well-maintained site actually costs beyond the initial build. A larger site or one with more moving parts scales up from there naturally.

## Why this matters when comparing quotes

Two developers quoting the same build price can leave you with very different total costs over three years, depending on what platform they build on and what ongoing fees come bundled with it by default. Ask specifically what a realistic yearly cost looks like after launch, not just the number for the initial build. That's the number that actually determines what a website costs you over time, not the headline price you see first.

Ongoing maintenance here is a stated flat monthly rate, no surprise invoices, listed right alongside the build pricing.
