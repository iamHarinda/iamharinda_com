A website quote often reads like a one-time cost — pay once, own it forever. In reality, a live website has ongoing costs baked into what it is, and not knowing about them upfront is how business owners end up surprised a year later.

## The costs that continue regardless of platform

- **Domain renewal** — typically $10–$20 a year, due annually or can be paid multiple years ahead. Miss this and your site can go down entirely, or worse, the domain can become available for someone else to register.
- **Hosting** — where the site's files actually live, billed monthly or annually. Ranges from a few dollars a month for a simple site to significantly more for something with heavier traffic or custom infrastructure.
- **SSL certificate** — the security certificate that makes a site load as "https" instead of showing a browser warning. Often bundled free with modern hosting, but worth confirming it's actually included and auto-renewing.

## Costs specific to certain platforms

- **WordPress:** premium plugin subscriptions (a booking plugin, a form builder, SEO tools), theme licence renewals, and periodically, developer time to handle plugin conflicts or updates.
- **Website builders (Wix, Squarespace):** a monthly or annual platform subscription that covers hosting bundled in, but locks you to that platform's pricing and any price increases over time.
- **Custom-coded sites:** generally the fewest ongoing platform fees, since there's no licensing or subscription tied to the code itself — mainly just hosting and the domain.

## The cost that's easy to underestimate: your own time or a maintenance plan

Content needs updating. Security patches need applying, especially on WordPress. Something occasionally breaks and needs fixing. This is either your own time (if you're comfortable handling it) or a maintenance arrangement with a developer, typically a flat monthly rate that covers routine updates and small fixes without a separate invoice every time something comes up.

## A realistic yearly budget range

For a straightforward small business site: domain ($10–$20/year) plus hosting ($60–$300/year depending on platform and traffic) plus, if you want it, a maintenance plan (commonly $100–$300/month depending on scope) covers most of what a well-maintained site actually costs beyond the initial build. A larger site or one with more moving parts scales up from there.

## Why this matters when comparing quotes

Two developers quoting the same build price can leave you with very different total costs over three years, depending on what platform they build on and what ongoing fees come bundled with it. Ask specifically what the realistic yearly cost looks like after launch, not just the number for the initial build — that's the number that actually determines what a website costs you over time.
