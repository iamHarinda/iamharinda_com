Of all the confusion in the apparel world, this might be the most common, and the most frustrating once it happens: "pattern design" and "patternmaking" sound like the same profession, and they are genuinely different jobs, done by different specialists, producing completely different deliverables.

## Pattern design (also called print or surface pattern design)

This is graphic and artistic work — designing the artwork, motifs, and repeat patterns that appear as a print or embroidery on a garment's surface. A pattern designer works in tools like Illustrator and Photoshop, producing print-ready artwork files and realistic mockups showing the design on the actual garment. This is design for the *surface* of the garment — what you see printed or embroidered on it.

## Patternmaking (technical or cut-and-sew patterns)

This is a completely different discipline — creating the actual technical templates used to cut fabric pieces that get sewn together into a garment's physical shape. A patternmaker works with garment construction, sizing and grading (scaling a pattern across a size range), seam allowances, and fit — the *structure* of the garment itself, not its surface decoration. This is what's needed to actually manufacture a garment from fabric.

## Why the confusion happens

Both use the word "pattern," both are technically part of "fashion design" broadly, and both involve specialized software and technical skill — but a pattern designer generally can't create a cut-and-sew technical pattern, and a patternmaker generally isn't the person to design a repeat print or a logo placement. They're adjacent fields that occasionally overlap in a large studio, but are usually entirely separate specialists.

## Why this matters for what you order

If you need artwork, prints, or a design placed on an existing garment (a polo, a t-shirt, a hoodie you're sourcing blank from a supplier) — that's pattern design. If you need the actual construction pattern to manufacture a new garment shape from scratch — cutting templates, grading across sizes, seam construction — that's patternmaking, and a completely different service.

Ordering pattern design when you actually needed patternmaking (or the reverse) wastes time and money on both sides — the work delivered technically fulfills what was asked for, but doesn't solve the actual problem, because the wrong specialty was hired for the job.

## How to tell which one you need

Ask yourself: am I sourcing a blank garment and need something designed onto its surface (print, pattern, logo placement)? That's pattern design. Am I creating a new garment shape from raw fabric and need the technical construction templates? That's patternmaking. If you're not sure, say what you're actually trying to achieve and a legitimate designer should tell you honestly whether it's a fit for their specialty or not.
