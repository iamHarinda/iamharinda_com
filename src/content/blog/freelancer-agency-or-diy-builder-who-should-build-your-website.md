There's no universally correct answer to "should I hire a freelancer, hire an agency, or just build it myself?" — the right choice depends on your budget, your timeline, and how much ongoing involvement you want. Here's what each path actually looks like in practice.

## DIY with a builder

You do the work yourself, using a platform like Wix or Squarespace. Cheapest option by far, and the fastest to get something live. The tradeoff is your own time — building a genuinely good-looking, well-organized site takes longer than most people expect on the first attempt, and you're limited to what the builder's editor supports.

**Best for:** very tight budgets, businesses testing an idea before committing further, or owners who enjoy the hands-on work and have the time for it.

## A freelancer

One person, usually more affordable than an agency because there's no team overhead, and often more direct communication — you're talking to the person actually doing the work, not an account manager relaying to a developer. The tradeoff: capacity is limited to one person, so very large or urgent projects can take longer, and if that person becomes unavailable mid-project, there's no backup.

**Best for:** most small businesses — a marketing site, a small business site with a CMS, or a modest web application, where direct communication and lower cost matter more than a large team's bandwidth.

## An agency

A team, usually with more capacity to handle larger or more complex projects, and often more structured processes (project managers, dedicated designers). The tradeoff: higher cost, since you're paying for more people and more overhead, and communication often goes through an account manager rather than directly with whoever's building the site.

**Best for:** larger projects, tight enterprise-level deadlines, or businesses that specifically want a dedicated point of contact managing multiple people rather than working with one person directly.

## A practical way to decide

Ask yourself three questions: How complex is what I actually need (a five-page site vs. a full custom application)? How much am I comfortable spending? And do I want to build this myself, work directly with one person, or have a team manage it for me? Most small businesses land on either a builder (for a very simple first site) or a freelancer (for something that needs to look genuinely professional and possibly do more than a builder allows) — an agency tends to make sense once the project's scope or urgency genuinely requires a team.
