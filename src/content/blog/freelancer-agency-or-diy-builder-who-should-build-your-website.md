There's no universally correct answer to "should I hire a freelancer, hire an agency, or just build it myself?" The right choice depends on your budget, your timeline, and how much ongoing involvement you actually want in running the thing afterward. Here's what each path really looks like once you're in it.

## DIY with a builder

You do the work yourself, using a platform like Wix or Squarespace. It's the cheapest option by a wide margin, and the fastest way to get something live today. The tradeoff is your own time. Building a genuinely good-looking, well-organized site takes longer than most people expect on their first attempt, and you're limited to whatever the builder's editor happens to support.

This fits very tight budgets, businesses testing an idea before committing further money, or owners who actually enjoy the hands-on work and have the time to spend on it.

## A freelancer

One person, usually more affordable than an agency since there's no team overhead sitting behind the price, and often more direct communication, since you're talking straight to the person actually doing the work, not an account manager relaying messages to a developer somewhere else. The tradeoff is that capacity is limited to one person, so very large or urgent projects can take longer, and if that person becomes unavailable mid-project, there's no backup waiting in the wings.

This fits most small businesses well, a marketing site, a small business site with a CMS, a modest web application, where direct communication and lower cost matter more than a large team's bandwidth.

## An agency

A team, usually with more capacity to handle larger or more complex projects, and often more structured processes, project managers, dedicated designers, all working in parallel. The tradeoff is a higher cost, since you're paying for more people and more overhead layered into every invoice, and communication often goes through an account manager rather than straight to whoever's actually building your site.

This fits larger projects, tight enterprise-level deadlines, or businesses that specifically want a dedicated point of contact managing several people rather than working directly with one person the whole way through.

## A practical way to actually decide

Ask yourself three things. How complex is what I actually need, a five-page site versus a full custom application? How much am I comfortable spending, realistically? And do I want to build this myself, work directly with one person, or have a team manage it for me start to finish? Most small businesses land on either a builder, for a very simple first site, or a freelancer, for something that needs to look genuinely professional and possibly do more than a builder allows. An agency tends to make sense once a project's scope or urgency genuinely requires an actual team behind it.

## One thing worth remembering

Price alone shouldn't decide this for you. A freelancer who's a poor fit for a complex project can cost more in wasted time than a slightly pricier agency that gets it right the first time around.

Working directly with the person actually building your site, with fixed pricing from a free scope call? That's the freelancer path, and it's what this is built around.
