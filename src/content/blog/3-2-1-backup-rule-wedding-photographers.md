A wedding happens exactly once. There's no reshoot if a hard drive fails, a laptop gets stolen, or a memory card corrupts halfway through offloading. That single fact is why backup strategy gets treated so seriously in this specific corner of photography, and the 3-2-1 rule is the simplest framework that actually holds up under real-world failure.

## What the 3-2-1 rule actually means

Keep three total copies of every file. Store those copies on two different types of media, not just three drives from the same manufacturer bought on the same day, since a manufacturing flaw or a specific failure mode can take out multiple drives from the same batch at once. Keep one copy somewhere physically separate from the other two, a cloud backup, a drive at a different location, so a fire, theft, or flood at your main workspace doesn't wipe out everything simultaneously.

## Why "one backup" isn't actually a backup

A single external hard drive holding your only copy of a wedding gallery isn't a backup, it's just where the files happen to live, with a single point of failure between you and disaster. Drives fail. It's not a question of if, it's a question of when, and the failure rate climbs the longer a drive stays in regular use. Treating one drive as sufficient is the single most common mistake in this whole topic.

## What redundancy looks like on shoot day itself

Cameras with dual card slots let you write to two cards simultaneously the moment a photo is captured, meaning you walk away from the ceremony with two physical copies before you've even opened a laptop. This is the cheapest, earliest point in the whole workflow to build in redundancy, and it's why dual card slots matter enough to factor into a camera body purchase specifically for wedding work.

## The offload step that actually protects you

Once you're back from the shoot, copy files to at least two separate drives before formatting any memory cards. A common, safe pattern: copy to your main working drive and a second backup drive at the same time, verify both copies actually completed and aren't corrupted, and only then consider the memory card safe to reuse. Skipping the verification step and trusting a copy "probably" went through is how people lose files they assumed were safe.

## Where cloud backup fits into this

A cloud backup service serves as your off-site copy, the "1" in 3-2-1, protecting against anything that could take out your physical location entirely. It doesn't need to be your primary working copy, since RAW files are large and cloud upload speeds vary, but having client galleries eventually land in cloud storage closes the single biggest gap in a purely local backup setup.

## How long to actually keep client files

Practices vary, but a common approach is keeping full-resolution originals for at least a year after delivery, sometimes longer, in case a client's own copy gets lost and they come back asking for a reprint or a re-download. Beyond that window, some photographers keep only the final edited selects rather than every RAW file, since storage costs add up across a full career of galleries.

## What this actually costs to set up properly

A genuine 3-2-1 setup for an active wedding photographer runs a few hundred dollars for local drives plus a modest monthly cloud subscription, a real but manageable cost against what a single lost wedding gallery would actually cost in refunds, reputation, and the impossible task of explaining to a couple that their day is simply gone.

## A backup habit worth building regardless of tools

Set a fixed point in your workflow, immediately after every shoot, before anything else, where backup happens without exception, rather than treating it as something to get to eventually. The photographers who lose files almost always trace it back to a backup step that got delayed "just this once" during a busy week.

## Why this matters even before editing starts

Whether you edit everything yourself or send files out, a solid backup means the RAW files handed over for editing are never your only copy of that wedding. That peace of mind is worth building before you ever need it, and it's exactly the kind of foundation that makes [outsourcing part of your editing](/pricing/#photo-editing) a low-risk decision rather than a nerve-wracking one.
