AI editing tools have gotten good fast — good enough that "should I just use AI for this shoot" is now a real question, not an obvious no. The honest answer is that it depends on what the photos are for.

## Where AI genuinely earns its place

Batch AI presets are excellent at consistent, low-stakes volume: a quick social media crop, a same-day sneak peek gallery, or a first pass on a shoot where the client only needs a general impression fast. They apply one look instantly across hundreds of frames, and for that narrow job, they do it well.

## Where AI tends to fall short

The problems show up in exactly the situations wedding and portrait photographers deal with most:

**Mixed lighting.** A reception with warm tungsten uplighting, cool window light, and a flash-lit dance floor confuses automatic white balance. A preset applies one correction across the whole set; a person adjusts each frame to what the light actually was, so skin reads the same in the ballroom shot as it does by the window.

**Skin.** AI skin smoothing tends to average texture away — pores and fine detail disappear along with blemishes, and the result reads as plastic rather than retouched. A person removes what is actually a distraction and leaves the rest, because skin without any texture reads as fake, not flawless.

**Judgment calls.** A slightly underexposed frame that is the only shot of a specific moment — the ring exchange, a candid reaction — needs a decision, not a formula: how far to push recovery before it looks pushed. AI applies the same rule everywhere; a person treats that frame differently because it matters differently.

**Consistency across a full gallery.** A preset can make every photo say "recently edited with the same tool." It cannot make photo 4 and photo 140 look like they belong to the same story the way a person grading the whole set with the earlier frames in mind can.

## A practical way to decide

If the deliverable is disposable or immediate — a story post, a same-day teaser — AI editing is the right tool and there is no reason to pay for more. If the deliverable is the final album or gallery the client keeps and shows people for years, that is where the gap between "a filter was applied" and "someone looked at this" tends to show.
