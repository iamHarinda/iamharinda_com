Every wedding photographer in the US has the same conversation with themselves at some point: keep editing every gallery by hand, or hand some of it to AI. In 2026 the honest answer is "both" — but knowing which photos go where is what actually saves your business time and your style.

## What AI editing is good at

AI tools like Imagen or Aftershoot are built for volume. Feed them a few thousand RAW files from a wedding day and they'll apply consistent exposure and colour corrections across the whole set in minutes, learning your general preferences as you go. For a photographer shooting forty weddings a year, that's real time back.

AI is a good fit when:

- The gallery is large (hundreds to thousands of images) and needs a first pass of basic corrections.
- The look is fairly consistent from frame to frame — a single venue, similar lighting.
- Turnaround speed matters more than a hand-checked result on every single image.

## Where AI still falls short

AI editing works from patterns, not judgment. It doesn't know that the bride's dress needs to stay true white instead of drifting warm under tungsten lighting, or that a groom's skin tone looks wrong next to a stone church wall. It also can't tell you which twenty photos in a four-hundred-image gallery are the ones that will end up printed and framed — the ones worth a closer look.

Human editing still wins when:

- Mixed or difficult lighting needs judgment calls, not just an algorithm's best guess.
- Skin tones have to hold up next to each other across the whole gallery, not just within one AI-detected "scene."
- The photographer's clients expect a specific, consistent style that a preset can approximate but not truly replicate.
- A small number of hero images need real retouching — not just a colour pass.

## The hybrid workflow most studios use now

Most photographers who outsource in 2026 don't pick one or the other. A common split: let AI or a bulk-editing service handle the first colour pass on the full gallery, then send the images that matter most — engagement portraits, the first dance, family formals — to a human editor for a closer, hand-checked edit. It keeps costs down without gambling the shots that actually get printed.

If your studio doesn't use AI presets at all and you edit everything from RAW by eye on a calibrated screen, that's also a completely reasonable choice — it just means turnaround is priced by volume rather than by the minute, and every frame gets looked at individually rather than batch-processed.

## How to decide for your own galleries

Ask three questions before you outsource a shoot:

1. **How many photos, and how similar is the lighting across them?** Large, consistent sets are where AI saves the most time.
2. **Which images will your client actually print or frame?** Those are worth a hand edit regardless of how the rest of the gallery is handled.
3. **Does your brand promise a specific, hand-crafted look?** If your marketing leans on "no AI, no presets," your editing has to match that promise all the way through delivery.

There's no wrong answer here — just a mismatch to avoid. Photographers who outsource fastest are the ones who match the editing method to what the gallery actually needs, instead of picking one approach for everything.
