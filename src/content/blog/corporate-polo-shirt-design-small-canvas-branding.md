A website has scroll space. A brochure has multiple pages. A corporate polo shirt has, realistically, a few square inches on the chest and maybe a larger back panel — a genuinely small, fixed canvas that has to represent your brand clearly, at a glance, from a normal conversational distance. That constraint shapes the design in specific ways.

## Simplify before you shrink

A logo with fine detail, small text, or subtle gradients that reads perfectly on a business card or website often loses that detail entirely once reduced to embroidery or small-scale print size. Rather than shrinking a complex logo and hoping it holds up, a simplified version — built specifically for small-scale reproduction — usually reads far more clearly at the actual size it'll be seen.

## Decide what actually needs to be on the shirt

Not every brand element needs to appear on the polo. A full logo lockup with a tagline, a wordmark, and a symbol might work on a letterhead, but a polo often reads better with just the symbol or a simplified mark — the full identity doesn't need to be crammed into a three-inch chest placement.

## Colour contrast against the actual garment

A logo that works on a white background can lose all contrast on navy, and vice versa. For a corporate order, this often means having a version of the logo specifically adapted for the garment colour being used — not just placing the standard logo file as-is and hoping it works.

## Consistency across a range of garment colours

If your company orders polos in more than one colour (which is common — different colours for different roles or seasons), the design needs a plan for how it adapts across all of them, not just the one colour it was originally designed against.

## Where the back panel fits in

A corporate order commonly uses the back panel for a larger, secondary element — a tagline, a larger version of the mark, sometimes a simple pattern that reinforces brand colours without needing to say anything specific. It's a genuinely different design job than the chest placement, even on the same garment, and benefits from being considered on its own rather than just scaled up from the chest version.

## The overall goal

A corporate polo design succeeds when someone can recognize the brand instantly, from a normal distance, without needing to read fine print or squint at detail that was never going to survive the reduction to garment scale in the first place.
