A large majority of couples say a quick sneak peek gallery matters to them — often more than the final delivery date, because the wait right after the wedding is when the anticipation is highest. The good news: delivering one fast doesn't require rushing the whole job. It requires treating it as a separate, smaller task.

## Why a sneak peek and a full gallery are different jobs

The instinct is to edit everything in shoot order and post whatever's done first. That's slow, because the first photos taken (getting-ready shots) usually aren't the ones a couple most wants to see right away — the first kiss, the rings, the reception entrance carry more emotional weight and are what typically gets shared. Treating the sneak peek as its own small project, selected deliberately rather than by chronology, is what makes the 48-hour target realistic.

## A workable process

1. **Select during culling, not after.** While culling the full shoot, flag 10–20 standout images as you go — the ones with the strongest emotional or visual impact. Don't wait until the full edit is done to go looking for them.
2. **Edit that small set first, completely.** Full colour correction and any retouching these specific images need — a sneak peek with a visible white balance issue undercuts the whole point.
3. **Deliver through a fast channel.** A quick online gallery link or even a shared folder works — the goal is speed of access, not a polished delivery presentation (that's what the full gallery is for).
4. **Continue the full edit on its normal timeline.** The sneak peek doesn't change how long the complete gallery takes; it just fills the waiting period with something meaningful.

## What this looks like when editing is outsourced

If editing is being handled by an outside service, ask specifically whether a small priority batch can be turned around faster than the full gallery — most services built around per-photo pricing can accommodate a 10–20 image priority request even while the rest of the gallery is still in progress, since it's a small, self-contained piece of work.

## Common mistakes that undercut a sneak peek

- **Choosing images by convenience** (whatever's already edited) instead of by impact — a technically fine but emotionally flat sneak peek doesn't generate the excitement it's meant to.
- **Over-editing under time pressure** — a rushed retouch on a hero image is worse than no retouch at all; keep the sneak peek edits simple and clean rather than ambitious.
- **Not telling the couple it's coming** — setting the expectation ("a small preview within 48 hours, the full gallery in [X] weeks") turns a nice surprise into a promise kept, which builds more trust than the surprise alone.
