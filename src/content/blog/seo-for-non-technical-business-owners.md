SEO (search engine optimization) sounds technical enough that a lot of business owners assume it's something only a specialist can understand, let alone do anything about. The core idea underneath the jargon is actually simple: it's everything that affects whether Google shows your site to someone searching for what you offer.

## What Google is actually trying to do

Google's job is to give someone searching "wedding photographer in Austin" the most relevant, trustworthy, easy-to-use results it can find. SEO, at its core, is making your site genuinely match what Google (and the person searching) is looking for — not tricking an algorithm, despite how it's sometimes described.

## The pieces that actually matter, without the jargon

- **Does your site clearly say what you do, and where?** A page that plainly states your service and location, in real sentences, tells Google (and visitors) more than clever wording ever will.
- **Is your site fast and usable on a phone?** Covered elsewhere, but it directly factors into ranking, not just visitor experience.
- **Do other reputable sites link to yours?** A mention or link from a local business directory, a partner, a press feature — these act as a kind of vote of credibility in Google's eyes.
- **Is your content actually useful, not just present?** A page that genuinely answers a real question (like this one) tends to perform better over time than a page that exists purely to contain keywords.
- **Is your Google Business Profile filled out and accurate?** For any business with a physical or service area, this is one of the highest-impact, entirely free things to get right.

## What SEO is not

It's not a one-time setting a developer flips on, and it's not something that produces results overnight — a brand new site typically takes a few months of consistent groundwork before organic search traffic becomes meaningful. It's also not primarily about sneaky tricks or keyword-stuffing; those approaches were penalized by Google's algorithm years ago and tend to backfire.

## What you can realistically do yourself, without hiring an SEO specialist

- Fill out and keep your Google Business Profile accurate and complete.
- Make sure your site's page titles and descriptions in plain language actually describe what's on each page.
- Add genuinely useful content over time — a blog, FAQ answers, service descriptions written for a real reader, not a search engine.
- Get listed in relevant local or industry directories where your business is legitimately a fit.

## When it's worth bringing in outside help

Technical SEO issues — a site that's structurally hard for Google to read, missing structured data, slow load times baked into the platform — are worth having someone with real expertise look at, since these aren't things you can meaningfully fix through content alone.
