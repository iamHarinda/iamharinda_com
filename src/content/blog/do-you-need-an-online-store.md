"Should my site let people buy directly, or just let them get in touch?" is a question worth answering honestly before a project starts, because full e-commerce is a meaningfully bigger, more expensive build than a site that simply presents what you offer and lets someone reach out.

## What a "contact me" site actually covers

A site built around inquiries — a contact form, a phone number, a booking link, maybe pricing shown as a starting range — works well for service-based businesses where every job is a bit different, or where you want a conversation before committing a customer to a price. Most consultants, photographers, contractors, and custom-work businesses fall here, even if they technically "sell" something.

## What a genuine online store adds

An online store needs: a product catalog with variations (size, colour, quantity), a shopping cart, secure payment processing, inventory tracking, shipping calculation, and usually order management and email confirmations. Each of those is a real feature, not a checkbox — which is why e-commerce sites typically cost more and take longer to build than a comparable informational site.

## A simple test

Ask: does a customer's purchase depend on a conversation, a custom quote, or scheduling — or is it a fixed product at a fixed price, ready to be bought the moment someone decides they want it? If it's the first, a contact-based site with clear pricing information is often genuinely better for conversion, not just cheaper — forcing a service business into a rigid checkout flow can create friction, not remove it. If it's the second — physical products, fixed-price digital downloads, prepaid packages — an actual store is the right tool.

## The middle ground: simple payment links

For businesses that sell a small number of fixed-price things (an online course, a handful of physical products, a package booking) without needing a full catalog, a simpler payment integration — a "buy now" button connected to a payment processor — can cover the need without the cost and complexity of a full e-commerce build. It's worth asking about specifically if a full store feels like overkill for what you actually sell.

## What this means for your budget

A basic informational or contact-driven site starts in the low thousands. A genuine e-commerce build, with product management and checkout, is a meaningfully larger project — both in upfront cost and in what it takes to maintain afterward (inventory, order fulfillment integration, payment processor fees). Being clear about which one you actually need, before requesting quotes, keeps you from either overpaying for capability you won't use or underbuilding something that can't do what your business needs.
