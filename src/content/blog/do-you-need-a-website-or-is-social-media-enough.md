A lot of small business owners get by for years on an Instagram page and a Google Business listing, and reasonably ask: do I actually need a website too? The honest answer is that social media and a website do different jobs, and skipping the second one costs you something specific, even if it isn't obvious day to day.

## What social media is genuinely good at

Instagram, Facebook and TikTok are built for discovery and quick engagement — someone scrolling sees your work, likes a post, maybe messages you. For visual businesses especially, that's real value, and it isn't something a website replaces.

## What it can't do

A social media page is rented space. The platform decides what people see, when your account can get flagged or restricted, and how your content is formatted — you can't control the layout, and you can't be found by someone searching Google for what you do unless they already know your account exists. It also doesn't look permanent: a page with no posts in six months reads as "maybe closed," even if you're still in business.

## What a website actually gives you that social doesn't

- **You own it.** No platform can suspend your account, change the algorithm, or redesign the interface overnight.
- **People can find you searching, not just scrolling.** Someone typing "wedding photographer near me" into Google is looking for exactly what you do, right now — that's a different kind of visitor than someone passively scrolling a feed.
- **It looks credible.** Fairly or not, a business with only a social page can read as smaller or less established than one with an actual website, especially to a customer who's never worked with you before.
- **It's where your Google Business listing actually points.** A "Website" link that goes to an Instagram profile is a small but real trust signal you're leaving on the table.

## When social media alone is genuinely fine

If you're purely referral-based, don't want new inbound customers you don't already know, or you're testing an idea before committing to it — social alone can be enough, at least for now. It's not that a website is mandatory for every business at every stage.

## The realistic middle ground

You don't need a huge site on day one. A handful of pages — what you do, who you are, how to contact you — covers most of what a first website needs to do. It doesn't replace your social accounts; it gives people who find you through search, word of mouth, or a business card a permanent, professional place to land that you control completely.
