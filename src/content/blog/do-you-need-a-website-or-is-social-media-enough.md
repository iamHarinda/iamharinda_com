A lot of small business owners get by for years on an Instagram page and a Google Business listing. Then they reasonably ask themselves, do I actually need a website too? The honest answer is that social media and a website do two different jobs. Skipping the second one costs you something real, even if it's not obvious on a normal day.

## What social media is genuinely good at

Instagram, Facebook, and TikTok are built for discovery and quick engagement. Someone scrolling sees your work, likes a post, maybe sends you a message. For visual businesses especially, that's real value, and nothing about a website replaces it.

## What it just can't do for you

A social media page is rented space. The platform decides what people see, when your account might get flagged or restricted, and how your content gets formatted. You can't control the layout, and you can't get found by someone searching Google for exactly what you do unless they already know your account exists in the first place. It also doesn't feel permanent. A page with no posts in six months reads as "maybe closed," even if you're still very much in business.

## What a website gives you that social just doesn't

You own it, full stop. No platform can suspend your account, tweak the algorithm overnight, or redesign the whole interface out from under you. People can find you by searching instead of just scrolling. Someone typing "wedding photographer near me" into Google is looking for exactly what you do, right now, and that's a completely different kind of visitor than someone passively scrolling a feed for entertainment.

It also looks more credible, fairly or not. A business with only a social page can read as smaller or less established than one with an actual website, especially to a customer who's never worked with you before and has nothing else to go on. And it's where your Google Business listing actually points. A "Website" link that just leads to an Instagram profile is a small trust signal you're leaving sitting on the table.

## When social media alone genuinely works fine

If you're purely referral-based, don't want new inbound customers you don't already know, or you're testing an idea before committing further, social alone can genuinely be enough, at least for now. A website isn't mandatory for every business at every stage of growth, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

## The realistic middle ground

You don't need a huge site on day one. A handful of pages, what you do, who you are, how to reach you, covers most of what a first website needs to accomplish. It doesn't replace your social accounts either. It gives people who find you through search, word of mouth, or a business card a permanent, professional place to land that you actually control from top to bottom.

## One more thing worth saying

A lot of business owners wait years to build a site because it feels like a big project. It doesn't have to be. Even a simple three or four page site, built once and left mostly alone, beats no website at all by a wide margin.
