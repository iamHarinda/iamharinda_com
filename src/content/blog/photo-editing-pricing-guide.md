If you shoot weddings, portraits or real estate and you are weighing whether to outsource editing, the first question is always the same: what does this actually cost? The honest answer is that it depends on the pricing model, and the market uses three of them.

## The three common pricing models

**Per photo.** You pay a fixed amount for every image delivered — usually somewhere between $0.10 and $0.50 per photo for standard colour correction and light retouching, more for heavy portrait retouching. This is the easiest model to budget against because the cost scales directly with the size of the shoot.

**Per hour.** The editor bills for time spent, typically $15–$40/hour depending on experience. This suits complex, unpredictable work — a single hero image that needs extensive compositing — but it is hard to quote in advance for a full shoot.

**Flat rate per shoot or package.** A fixed price for a set volume of photos, regardless of exactly how long each one takes. This is the most common model for wedding and event photographers because it makes cost predictable across shoots of similar size.

## What actually moves the price

- **Volume.** Bulk orders (100+ photos) almost always come at a lower per-photo rate than a handful of images, because the editor can work through a consistent set efficiently.
- **Complexity.** Straightforward colour correction and exposure work costs far less than detailed skin retouching, object removal, or compositing multiple exposures.
- **Turnaround.** A 24–48 hour rush typically costs more than a standard 3–5 day turnaround.
- **RAW vs JPEG.** Editors generally charge the same either way, but RAW files give more room to recover highlights, shadows and colour — which affects how much correction is even possible, separate from price.
- **Human vs AI-assisted editing.** Batch AI presets are cheap or free because they apply one look to every frame. A human editor judging each photo individually costs more, but catches what a preset misses — a colour cast from mixed lighting, a face that needs a different touch than the one next to it.

## A realistic price range

For colour correction and standard retouching, expect to pay somewhere in the range of $10–$40 per 50–200 photos from an independent editor, more from an agency with account management overhead built in. If a quote comes in far below that for genuinely hand-edited work, ask how it is being delivered — it is worth confirming whether a person is actually looking at each frame.
