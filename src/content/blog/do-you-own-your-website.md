It's an uncomfortable question to even think to ask. If you paid for your website, don't you automatically own it? Not always, and the moment this usually comes up is exactly the wrong time to find out, right when you're trying to switch developers or platforms and discover you can't actually take anything with you.

## Three things "ownership" actually covers

The domain. Is it registered under your name and your account, or under the developer's? If it's theirs, you don't control your own web address, full stop. They could, in theory, hold it hostage or simply lose track of it someday. The code. For a custom-built site, do you have access to the real source files, or does everything live only on the developer's own account somewhere? Without the code, you can't move the site anywhere else, even if you technically own the domain. The platform account. If the site runs on a builder like Wix or Squarespace, or a hosting account, is it under your business's login, or somebody's personal one you've never even seen?

You can technically "own" a website in the sense that you paid for it, while controlling none of these three things in practice, which means you don't actually control your own website at all.

## Why this happens so often

It's often not malicious. A freelancer sets everything up under their own accounts because it's faster during the build, and the handover just never happens once the project wraps. But it also happens because some agencies and platforms deliberately keep you dependent, since a locked-in client is a lot easier to keep billing month after month.

## What to check right now if you already have a website

Log into your domain registrar account. Is your business's own name and email listed as the owner, not a developer's? Ask whoever built your site for the source code or repository, if it's custom-built. A legitimate answer is either "here it is" or a clear explanation of what platform it's on and how you can access your own account for it directly. Confirm hosting is billed to an account you control, not a reseller markup running through someone else's account you have no visibility into.

## What to insist on before your next website gets built

Ownership of the domain, the code or full account access if it's platform-based, and the hosting account should transfer to you as a normal part of launch, not something you have to request, negotiate, or pay extra for after the fact. If a developer hesitates or gets vague about committing to that upfront, that's worth taking seriously before you sign anything at all.

## A final word on this

This isn't about distrust. Most developers handle this fine without ever being asked. But asking upfront costs you nothing and protects you completely if things ever go sideways down the road.

Full code ownership, domain in your name, no lock-in, that's the standard here, not an upsell you have to pay extra for. Book a scope call, or reach out if you want to check your current setup.
