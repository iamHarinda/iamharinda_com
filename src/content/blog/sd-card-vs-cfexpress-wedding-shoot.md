CFexpress cards get talked about like the obvious upgrade every serious photographer needs, and the marketing around them leans hard into speed numbers that sound impressive without explaining whether that speed actually solves a problem you have. For a lot of wedding photographers, a good SD card still does the job completely fine.

## What CFexpress actually offers

CFexpress cards deliver dramatically faster read and write speeds than even the fastest SD cards, some pushing past 3000 megabytes per second compared to an SD card's several hundred. That speed genuinely matters for continuous high-speed burst shooting, twenty to thirty frames per second, where a slower card creates a buffer bottleneck and the camera has to pause and wait before it can keep shooting. It also matters for offloading large volumes of footage quickly at the end of a long day.

## Why most wedding photography doesn't need that speed

Wedding photography, even fast-paced moments like a first dance or a bouquet toss, rarely demands the sustained twenty-plus frames per second burst rates that make CFexpress speed genuinely necessary. Most wedding shooting happens at more moderate burst rates or single shots, well within what a good UHS-II SD card handles without any buffering slowdown at all.

## Where the SanDisk Extreme Pro fits

For photographers using a camera with an SD card slot, the SanDisk Extreme PRO UHS-II V90 remains a reliable, widely used choice, fast enough to keep up with moderate burst shooting and reliable enough that card failure during a wedding is genuinely rare when using a reputable brand rather than a bargain-bin alternative.

## When CFexpress actually earns its higher price

If you're shooting a camera body that only accepts CFexpress, some newer high-end mirrorless bodies have dropped SD slots entirely, the decision isn't really optional. If you regularly shoot high-speed sports-style bursts even at weddings, an especially fast first dance sequence, a ring toss caught mid-air, the buffer headroom genuinely helps. And if offload time at the end of a long wedding day matters enough that minutes saved translate into real value for your workflow, the speed differential adds up across a full season of shoots.

## The real cost difference worth knowing

CFexpress cards typically cost noticeably more per gigabyte than equivalent SD cards, sometimes two to three times as much for comparable capacity. For a photographer shooting several weddings a month, that difference across multiple cards adds up to a real ongoing cost, one worth weighing against whether the speed is solving an actual bottleneck or just sounding impressive on a spec sheet.

## What actually matters more than the speed number

Reliability and build quality matter more than raw speed for most wedding use. A card with genuine dust and water resistance, from an established brand with a track record in professional use, protects against the failure modes that actually threaten a wedding gallery, corruption, physical damage, counterfeit cards from unreliable sellers, far more than an extra thousand megabytes per second of theoretical throughput.

## A simple way to decide for your own setup

Check what your specific camera body actually supports first, since that sometimes settles the question outright. If your camera takes SD cards and you're not consistently maxing out the buffer during real shoots, a quality UHS-II SD card is a completely reasonable, cost-effective choice. If you're hitting buffer limits regularly, or your body requires CFexpress, then the upgrade is solving a real problem rather than chasing a number.

## Avoiding counterfeit cards regardless of which type you choose

Counterfeit memory cards, sold at suspiciously low prices through unverified third-party sellers, are a genuine risk in this category, and a corrupted counterfeit card is far more dangerous than a slightly slower genuine one. Buy from authorized retailers or directly from the manufacturer, especially for anything holding an entire wedding day's irreplaceable files.

## A hybrid approach worth considering

Some photographers with cameras that support both slot types run a fast CFexpress card for the primary shooting slot and a reliable SD card as the instant backup slot, getting speed where it matters most while still keeping a second physical copy the moment a photo is taken. This isn't necessary for everyone, but it's a reasonable middle ground for anyone genuinely torn between the two formats rather than facing a clear-cut decision either way.

## Why chasing the newest card generation rarely pays off

Memory card technology keeps advancing, and there's always a slightly faster, slightly newer generation available at a premium price. For working wedding photographers, a card that's a generation or two behind the absolute cutting edge, from a reputable brand, at a proven reliability track record, is almost always the smarter buy than paying extra to be first in line for marginal speed gains that don't address an actual bottleneck in your workflow.
