At a certain booking volume, every wedding photographer hits the same fork: the next hire either helps on shoot day or helps after it. A second shooter and an outsourced editor solve genuinely different problems, and picking the wrong one first is a common way to spend money without fixing the actual bottleneck.

## What a second shooter actually buys

A second shooter adds coverage, not time. They let you:

- Cover more of a wedding day simultaneously (getting-ready on both sides, multiple ceremony angles).
- Take on larger or more complex weddings without turning down other bookings for capacity reasons alone.
- Reduce risk — a backup shooter if something goes wrong with the primary.

What a second shooter doesn't do: touch your editing backlog. Every extra photo they take still needs to be culled and edited by the same process as before — often making the post-production bottleneck worse, not better, since there are now more images per wedding.

## What outsourced editing actually buys

Outsourcing editing (in full, or the standard-correction portion of it) buys back hours, not coverage. It lets you:

- Take on more weddings per month without extending how long each one takes to deliver.
- Keep evenings and weekends for shooting and client relationships instead of a backlog of Develop module work.
- Turn variable post-production time into a predictable, priced cost per gallery, which makes quoting new bookings more straightforward.

What it doesn't do: add coverage capacity on the actual wedding day.

## A simple way to tell which bottleneck you actually have

Ask which of these is currently true:

- **"I'm turning down bookings because I don't have anyone available to shoot that date."** → That's a coverage problem. A second shooter (or associate photographer) is the more direct fix.
- **"I'm booking fine, but deliveries keep slipping and I'm editing until midnight most nights."** → That's a post-production problem. Outsourcing editing addresses it directly; adding a second shooter would make it worse.
- **"Both."** → Common at higher volumes. Most studios that scale past a certain point end up doing both — an associate or second shooter for coverage, and an outsourced or in-house editing team for post-production — because trying to solve a capacity problem by working faster on both ends eventually runs into the same wall: there are only so many hours in a week.

## The cost comparison isn't quite apples to apples

A second shooter is usually a per-event flat fee or day rate. Outsourced editing is usually priced per photo or per gallery. Comparing them head to head on cost misses the point — they're not substitutes for each other, they're fixes for different constraints. The right question isn't "which is cheaper," it's "which constraint is actually limiting the business right now."
