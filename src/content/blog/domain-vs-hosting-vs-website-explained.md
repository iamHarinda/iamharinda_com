If you've ever been quoted separately for a "domain," "hosting," and a "website" and wondered why those aren't the same thing, you're not missing something obvious — they genuinely are three separate pieces, and understanding the difference makes every other website conversation easier.

## The domain: your address

A domain (like yourbusiness.com) is your website's address — what someone types into a browser, or what shows up in a Google search result. You don't buy a domain outright; you rent it, typically year to year, from a domain registrar. It's usually the cheapest of the three pieces — often $10–$20 a year.

Owning the domain is what actually matters long-term. As long as you personally own the domain (not a developer or agency on your behalf), you can move your website anywhere without losing your address.

## Hosting: where the website actually lives

Hosting is server space — a computer, somewhere, that stores your website's files and serves them to anyone who visits. Without hosting, a website exists as files on a hard drive, not as something people can actually see online. Hosting is an ongoing cost, typically billed monthly or annually, and it's separate from both the domain and the cost of actually building the site.

## The website: what's actually on the hosting

The website itself — the pages, the design, the content — is what gets uploaded to hosting and made accessible through the domain. Building it is usually a one-time cost (or a project cost, for something more complex); hosting and the domain are ongoing costs that continue for as long as the site is live.

## How they connect

Think of it like a shop: the domain is the street address, hosting is the physical building, and the website is what's actually inside it. You need all three for a functioning site — a domain with no hosting points to nothing, hosting with no domain has no address anyone can find, and a website with neither exists only on someone's computer.

## Why this matters when you're getting quotes

A quote that only covers "the website" and doesn't mention hosting or the domain is missing two of the three pieces you'll actually need to pay for. Ask specifically: is hosting included, for how long, and who owns the domain registration — your name, or the developer's. That last question matters more than it sounds; a domain registered under someone else's account can make it genuinely difficult to move your site later.
