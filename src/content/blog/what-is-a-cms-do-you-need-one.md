"Do you want a CMS?" It's a question a lot of business owners get asked without ever getting a plain answer to what it actually means. A CMS, short for content management system, is simply a way to edit your website's content, text, images, blog posts, product listings, without needing to know how to code or touch the underlying files directly at all.

## What having a CMS actually looks like day to day

You log into a private dashboard, click on a page or post, edit the text or swap out an image the way you'd edit a document, and publish the change. It's live on your site within seconds, no developer involved anywhere in the process. WordPress's admin panel is the most familiar example most people have seen. There are also lighter, more modern "headless" CMS options that plug into a custom-coded site the same basic way.

## When you genuinely need one

You'll be updating content regularly, a blog, changing promotions, new products, updated hours or menus. More than one person needs to make edits, and you don't want every small change routed through a developer first. Your business changes often enough that a static site would look out of date within a few months of launching.

## When you probably don't

The site is mostly informational and rarely changes, a portfolio, a simple "who we are and how to reach us" page. Any update, however small, is fine to send to a developer rather than doing it yourself. Some businesses genuinely prefer this setup, since it means one fewer system to learn and maintain. You're prioritizing the fastest, cleanest possible site, and a CMS would add complexity you don't actually need yet at this stage.

## The tradeoff nobody mentions upfront

A CMS adds real value if you'll actually use it, but it also adds a layer that needs maintaining, software updates, a login to keep secure, sometimes a small ongoing cost tacked on. A site without one is simpler and often faster to load, precisely because there's less running behind the scenes to slow things down. Neither option is objectively better. The right choice depends entirely on how often you expect to touch the content yourself.

## A middle ground worth knowing about

Modern headless CMS setups let you get the editing convenience, log in, change text, publish, without tying the whole site to a heavier platform underneath it. It's a reasonable way to get real editing ability without taking on everything that comes bundled with a full platform like WordPress.

## The question to actually ask when you're getting quoted

Not "do I need a CMS" in the abstract, but "how often will I realistically want to change something on this site myself, without asking someone else to do it for me?" That answer, more than anything else, determines whether a CMS is worth the added setup and ongoing upkeep.

Not sure whether your site needs a CMS or not? That's exactly the kind of thing a scope call sorts out before any pricing even gets discussed.
