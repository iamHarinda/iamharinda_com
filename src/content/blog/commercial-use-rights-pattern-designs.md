It's a detail that's easy to overlook until it actually matters. Receiving a finished design file doesn't automatically mean you're legally clear to sell products using it. Commercial use rights, what you're actually permitted to do with a design once it's delivered, are a real, separate consideration from the design work itself.

## What "commercial use" actually means

Commercial use rights are the specific permissions granted alongside a design that determine whether you can use it to make money, selling products featuring it, using it in advertising, applying it across a whole product line, and so on, as opposed to using it purely for personal, non-commercial purposes only.

## Why this can be a real problem with the wrong source

Stock design elements, purchased clip art, or motifs pulled from generic pattern marketplaces sometimes come with limited licences, personal use only, a cap on how many units you can sell, or a requirement to buy an extended licence separately for commercial or large-scale use. A design built from elements under a restrictive licence can leave you technically not permitted to actually sell the products you're making with it, even though you paid good money for the design itself.

## What custom, commissioned design work should include

When a design gets created specifically for you, original artwork built from scratch for your brief, not assembled from someone else's licensed stock elements, commercial use rights should be included as standard, without a separate licence fee or usage cap tacked on afterward. You're not licensing someone else's pre-made asset. You're commissioning original work made for your specific use from the ground up.

## What to explicitly confirm before you order

Is the design being built from scratch, or does it incorporate any pre-made stock elements that might carry their own licensing terms attached? Does the price include full commercial use rights, with no cap on units produced or sold down the line? Can the design get used across multiple products, or is it licensed for one specific use only? Who retains the underlying rights to the design, do you have exclusive use, or could the same or a similar design get sold to someone else entirely?

## Why this matters more as your order gets bigger

For a single small batch of shirts, licensing ambiguity is a manageable risk you can probably live with. For a full product line, a large production run, or a design that's central to your brand, unclear commercial rights are a real liability, worth explicitly confirming in writing before committing to a large order, not just assumed based on having paid for the design.

## A simple rule of thumb

If a designer can't clearly answer whether you get full commercial rights, ask directly, in writing, before you order anything larger than a small test batch. A one-line confirmation now saves a real headache later.
