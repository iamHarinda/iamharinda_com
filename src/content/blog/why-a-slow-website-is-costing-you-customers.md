"Site speed" gets talked about like a technical nitpick — something for developers to worry about, not something that actually affects your business. In practice, it's one of the more direct ways a website loses you customers, and it doesn't take technical knowledge to understand why.

## What "slow" actually means to a visitor

Nobody consciously thinks "this page took 4.2 seconds to load." What they experience is a blank or half-loaded screen for what feels like too long, and a real share of visitors leave before it finishes — especially on mobile, where connections are less consistent and patience is shorter. That's a customer gone before they ever saw what you offer.

## Why it happens

Slow sites are usually slow for a small number of avoidable reasons:

- **Unoptimized images** — a photo straight from a camera or phone, uploaded at full size, can be many times larger than it needs to be for a web page.
- **Too many third-party scripts** — chat widgets, tracking pixels, embedded videos and fonts loaded from other services all add load time, and it's easy to accumulate more of them than a site actually needs.
- **Heavy website builders or bloated themes** — some platforms and pre-made themes load a large amount of code to support features a given site doesn't even use.
- **Cheap or oversold hosting** — hosting that's overloaded with too many other sites on the same server responds slower across the board.

## Why this matters beyond just visitor patience

Google also factors site speed into search rankings — a slow site can rank lower than a faster competitor with genuinely similar content, purely because of load time. Speed isn't just about keeping visitors from leaving; it affects whether people find you in the first place.

## What a fast site actually requires

- Images sized and compressed properly for the web, not uploaded at full camera resolution.
- Only the third-party scripts a site genuinely needs, not every tool that seemed useful at some point.
- Clean, purpose-built code rather than a heavy theme carrying features that go unused.
- Hosting suited to the actual site — a small brochure site doesn't need expensive infrastructure, but it does need hosting that isn't badly overloaded.

## What to check on your own site right now

Google's own free PageSpeed Insights tool (search "PageSpeed Insights") gives a plain-language readout of how your specific site performs and what's slowing it down — worth five minutes even without any technical background, since the report explains the issues in fairly accessible terms.

## The upside of getting this right

A genuinely fast site isn't a luxury feature — it's one of the more reliable ways to keep more of the visitors you're already attracting, without spending anything extra on getting new ones to replace the ones who left.
