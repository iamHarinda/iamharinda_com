Ordering a custom polo or golf shirt design without a design background can feel like you already need to know the right terminology just to explain what you want. You don't. You just need to be specific about a handful of practical things, in totally plain language you already use every day.

## What's this actually for?

A team, a company, a golf club, a personal brand, an event? The context shapes tone. A golf club crest and a startup's branded polo call for genuinely different design approaches, even with pretty similar technical requirements underneath.

## What colours are involved?

Your brand or team colours, and the garment colour, or colours, the design will actually be placed on. If you're not locked into specific colours yet, say that too. It just means colour becomes part of the first concept round instead of a fixed constraint from day one.

## Where does it go on the shirt?

Chest only, chest and back, a sleeve detail? Roughly how big, relative to the shirt, small and subtle, or a bold, large placement that grabs attention?

## What's the vibe you're going for?

You don't need design vocabulary here at all. Plain description works completely fine. Bold and eye-catching, clean and professional, a bit playful, premium and understated. A couple of reference designs you like, from anywhere, another brand, a photo, a Pinterest board, communicate this even more clearly than words alone ever could.

## What do you already have?

An existing logo, brand colours, a mascot or symbol you want incorporated somehow, send whatever exists, even if it's rough around the edges. If you're starting from nothing at all, that's a completely normal starting point too, just worth mentioning upfront so nobody's confused.

## How will it actually be produced?

Embroidery, screen print, sublimation, if you know. If you don't know yet, that's fine, but mention it as soon as you find out, since it genuinely affects how the design should get built from the start.

## How many designs or concepts do you want to see?

Some orders want just one polished concept refined through revisions. Others want a few different directions to choose between before committing to one path. Either is reasonable, just be clear about which one you're expecting going in.

## Putting it all together

"This is for our golf club, navy and white colours. We want a small embroidered version on the chest and a bolder printed design on the back. We like clean, modern golf branding, similar feel to this reference. We have a logo already, attached. This will be embroidered on the chest, screen printed on the back."

That's a complete, plain-language brief, no design terminology required anywhere, and everything a designer needs for an accurate first concept right out of the gate.

Have a rough idea but don't know how to describe it well? Message first and talk it through before ordering anything, that's exactly what the process is built for.
