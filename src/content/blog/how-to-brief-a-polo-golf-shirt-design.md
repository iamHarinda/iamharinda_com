Ordering a custom polo or golf shirt design without a design background can feel like you need to already know the right terminology to explain what you want. You don't — you just need to be specific about a handful of practical things, in plain language.

## 1. What's this for?

A team, a company, a golf club, a personal brand, an event? The context shapes tone — a golf club crest and a startup's branded polo call for genuinely different design approaches, even with similar technical requirements.

## 2. What colours are involved?

Your brand or team colours, and the garment colour(s) the design will actually be placed on. If you're not locked into specific colours yet, say that too — it just means colour becomes part of the first concept round rather than a fixed constraint.

## 3. Where does it go on the shirt?

Chest only, chest and back, a sleeve detail? Roughly how big, relative to the shirt — small and subtle, or a bold, large placement?

## 4. What's the vibe?

You don't need design vocabulary here — plain description works fine: "bold and eye-catching," "clean and professional," "a bit playful," "premium and understated." A couple of reference designs you like (from anywhere — another brand, a photo, a Pinterest board) communicate this even more clearly than words alone.

## 5. What do you already have?

An existing logo, brand colours, a mascot or symbol you want incorporated — send whatever exists, even if it's rough. If you're starting from nothing, that's a completely normal starting point too, just worth saying upfront.

## 6. How will it be produced?

Embroidery, screen print, sublimation — if you know. If you don't know yet, that's fine, but mention it as soon as you find out, since it genuinely affects how the design should be built.

## 7. How many designs or concepts do you want to see?

Some orders want just one polished concept refined through revisions; others want a few different directions to choose between before committing to one. Either is reasonable — just be clear about which you're expecting.

## Putting it together

"This is for our golf club, navy and white colours. We want a small embroidered version on the chest and a bolder printed design on the back. We like clean, modern golf branding — similar feel to [reference]. We have a logo already, attached. This will be embroidered on the chest, screen printed on the back."

That's a complete, plain-language brief — no design terminology required, and everything a designer needs for an accurate first concept.
